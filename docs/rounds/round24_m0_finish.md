## 2. Numbers (obs.)

**O0 three-bus example** (A cheap, B dear, 150 MW at C, A–C limited to 80 MW), solved and checked:
- dispatch 90 / 60 MW; cost $4,800;
- LMPs **$20 / $50 / $80**; A–C shadow price $90/MWh;
- matched four ways:
  - the hand derivation;
  - brute force: the cost rises by exactly the LMP for one more MW at each bus;
  - relaxing the limit by 1 MW saves exactly $90;
  - the LMPs are unchanged when the reference bus moves.

**Benchmarks** (this container: Linux, 4 cores, Python 3.13.17):

|---|---|---|
| trailing 1-in-100 loss on 3,858 days | 0.40 s | 9,500 days/s |
| sized equity path, 3,858 days | 0.0018 s | 2.1 M days/s |
| one capital summary | 16 µs | 61,000 per s |

The trailing p99 is the slow one: one sort per day. It runs once per data build, not once per trader, so it doesn't touch the §14.3 evolution target. It is a candidate for an incremental window at M3 if profiling says so.

**A discrepancy in `capital_sizing.md` §10:**
- §10 gives **$0.195**/MWh at $1.05M and cleared share 1.0.
- The formula gives 50,000 / (0.75277 × 936 × 365) = **$0.1944**.
- §10's own $0.270 at share 0.72 needs $0.1944 (0.195 / 0.72 would be $0.271).

So $0.195 is a rounding slip in the original document. The test uses $0.1944, and `capital_sizing.md` is left as received.
